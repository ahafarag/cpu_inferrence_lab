Explain why the sky is blue and why sunsets are red, assuming the reader has taken one year of high school physics. Start with the idea that sunlight contains all visible wavelengths, then describe how molecules in the atmosphere scatter short wavelengths far more strongly than long ones, with the scattering strength rising roughly as the inverse fourth power of the wavelength. Explain why we do not see a violet sky even though violet is scattered most, mentioning both the solar spectrum and the sensitivity of the human eye. Then describe what changes at sunset: the light travels through a much longer path of atmosphere, most of the blue is scattered out of the beam before it reaches the observer, and what remains is orange and red. Finish with two common misconceptions, including the claim that the sky reflects the color of the ocean.
