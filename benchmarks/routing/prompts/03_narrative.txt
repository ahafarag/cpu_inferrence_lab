The ferry from the mainland was late again, and by the time Marta reached the harbour the market stalls were already folding their awnings against the wind. She had been away for eleven years, long enough that the bakery on the corner had become a pharmacy and the old fishing nets that once hung from the pier had been replaced by a row of cafes with names in three languages. Still, the smell of salt and diesel was exactly as she remembered it, and so was the way the gulls argued over scraps near the fish cleaning tables. Her grandmother's house stood at the top of the hill where the road narrowed into steps. The blue door had faded to the color of an old bruise, and when she turned the key she half expected the hinges to protest. Instead the door swung open without a sound, as if someone had been oiling it all along.
